# Pale Blue Mind — brand bible

**One line:** Big questions, answered with awe.

**What it is:** A faceless, cinematic science channel that makes the universe
feel close. Calm narration, slow stunning visuals, and a script that takes one
genuinely mind-bending question and walks you to the answer.

---

## 1. Positioning

There are three crowded lanes in science YouTube:

- **Hyper-produced explainers** (Kurzgesagt, Veritasium) — gorgeous, expensive,
  low frequency. Hard to compete on production; impossible to match cadence.
- **Talking-head science** — depends on a personality. Not automatable.
- **Low-effort faceless "fact" channels** — high volume, but interchangeable,
  thin, and algorithmically disposable.

**Pale Blue Mind sits in the open gap:** faceless and high-cadence like the
third lane, but with the *script quality and emotional payoff* of the first.
The moat is the writing — a strong hook, a real narrative arc, and a genuine
"wow" at the end — which is exactly what an LLM-driven pipeline can produce
consistently and at scale, where most faceless channels coast on filler.

### Target viewer

The "shower-thought" audience: curious adults (broad, 18–45 skew) who like the
*feeling* of understanding something vast, don't want a lecture, and watch
science content to feel awe and calm rather than to study. Massive, global,
and language-agnostic in appeal (translatable later).

---

## 2. Content pillars

Every video belongs to one pillar. Pillars keep the channel coherent (so
subscribers know what they're getting) while leaving topics infinite.

1. **Scale** — the size of things. "If the Sun were a grain of sand…",
   "What's the largest thing in the universe?" Reliable awe; great for Shorts.
2. **What if** — counterfactual physics. "What if the Moon disappeared?",
   "What happens if you fall into a black hole?" Highest retention; the hook
   writes itself.
3. **Deep time** — the past and future at cosmic timescales. "The last star",
   "Earth in 250 million years." Emotional, evergreen.
4. **Frontier** — the genuinely unknown. "Why is the universe expanding faster?",
   "What's inside a black hole?" Positions the channel as smart, not clickbait.
5. **Pale blue** — perspective pieces that zoom back to Earth and us. The
   emotional anchor of the brand; lower frequency, high shareability.

## 3. Formats

| Format | Length | Cadence | Job |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Shorts** | 30–60s | Daily | Top of funnel. One scale/what-if hook, one payoff. This is the discovery engine. |
| **Long-form** | 8–12 min | 2× / week | Watch-time + revenue + subscriber conversion. A single question, deep narrative arc. |
| **Perspective** | 4–6 min | ~1× / month | Brand-building, highly shareable "Pale blue" pieces. |

The pipeline produces both `short` and `long` formats; `script.ts` adapts
structure to the format.

### Long-form structure (the retention spine)

1. **Hook (0–15s)** — the question, framed so the viewer *must* know the
   answer. No channel intro, no "hey guys."
2. **Stakes (15–40s)** — why this question matters / why it's strange.
3. **Build (the body)** — 3–5 beats, each escalating, each with its own
   mini-payoff so the retention graph never flatlines.
4. **Payoff** — the awe moment. The answer, delivered with weight.
5. **Turn (last 20s)** — zoom back to the human/Earth scale ("Pale blue"
   signature), then a soft, specific CTA and a teaser for the next video.

## 4. Voice & visual identity

**Narration voice:** Calm, warm, precise. Curious, never condescending.
Short sentences. Lets silence and visuals breathe. Think a thoughtful friend
who happens to know the cosmos — not a documentary boomer, not a hype bro.

**Visual language:**
- Slow, drifting motion (space footage, nebulae, planetary surfaces, Earth).
- High contrast, deep blacks, a single warm accent (amber/gold) against cosmic
  blue. Minimal on-screen text — one phrase at a time, large.
- No frantic cuts. Cuts land on narration beats. Calm is the brand.

**Thumbnails:** One subject, one short bold phrase (3–5 words), high contrast,
readable at phone size. The thumbnail states the question; the video answers
it. Thumbnail and title must never overpromise what the video delivers.

**Audio bed:** Ambient, low, cinematic. Never competes with narration.

## 5. Monetization (sequenced)

Popularity first, revenue as a function of it. In order of unlock:

1. **YouTube Partner Program** — ads on long-form + Shorts once eligible
   (watch-hours / subs threshold). Science is a mid-to-decent CPM niche;
   long-form 8–12 min videos allow mid-roll ads.
2. **Affiliate** — telescopes, star projectors, science books, space toys.
   Naturally relevant; placed in descriptions, not forced into scripts.
3. **Digital products** — wallpapers, a "scale of the universe" poster set,
   ambient-space audio packs. Near-zero marginal cost, on-brand.
4. **Channel memberships / Patreon** — ad-free, early access, "name a star in
   the credits." Converts the awe into a community.
5. **Licensing & brand deals** — once the catalogue and audience exist,
   science/education sponsors and footage licensing.

The pipeline's job is to drive (1) — volume + quality + consistency — because
every other tier scales off audience size.

---

## 6. Guardrails (what keeps the channel alive)

Faceless, automated channels die from preventable mistakes. Hard rules:

- **Facts are grounded and reviewed.** Scripts must be checkable; every package
  is reviewed by a human before publish. The pipeline assists writing, it does
  not get final say on truth.
- **No duplicated or reused content.** Each script is original; topics are
  de-duplicated against the idea log.
- **Thumbnails match content.** No bait. The question on the thumbnail is the
  question the video answers.
- **Licensed visuals & audio only.** Stock via licensed APIs; respect terms.
- **Disclose AI where it matters** and keep sensitive "YMYL" claims (health,
  finance, etc.) out of scope — this channel stays in awe-and-wonder territory.
- **Publish private-first.** Upload defaults to `private`/scheduled; a human
  flips it public.

These aren't just ethics — they're the difference between a channel that
compounds for years and one that gets a strike and vanishes.
